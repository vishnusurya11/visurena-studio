# Skill: story analyst — what happens (analysis 02_02)

You record the EVENTS of a novel chapter, scene by scene (use the provided scene
breakdown's numbers). **The one law: asserted realis only — events the text asserts
actually happened (or are happening) in the story world.** Everything else is off
the list.

## The realis battery — an event must pass ALL four

1. **Positive**: "he did not understand" = NO event.
2. **Past/present**: futures, intentions, plans = NO.
3. **Specific**: a singular occurrence at a particular place and time. Habituals and
   generics ("he generally arrived on Sundays", "dogs bark") = NO.
4. **Asserted**: hypotheticals, wishes, fears, dreams, and a character's beliefs
   about what happened = NO (they belong to the believer, not the event list).

Also: figurative actions ("broke the ice", "her heart flew") are NOT literal events —
record the literal happening they convey, if any. Events that occurred BEFORE this
scene and are merely recalled are backstory — skip them here (a character LEARNING
about them now IS a current event of type `reporting`).

## What to record

Per event: `type` (`action` · `movement` — arrivals/departures/travel · `perception`
— saw/heard/found/noticed · `reporting` — said/told/asked as acts of speech ·
`other`), a one-line `summary`, `participants` (names as written), `paras` (anchor
paragraphs), and a **verbatim `quote`** from those paragraphs evidencing it.

Speech acts ARE events — "said/cried/asked" are the most frequent real events in
fiction. (What was said belongs to the dialogue layer; the act of saying is yours.)

State-y words count only when the change happens IN-scene ("he was elated on hearing
it") or the word is inherently acute ("stunned", "aghast") — standing descriptions
("stained and frayed apparel") do not.

## Granularity

Salient beats, not every verb: capture every event that moves the story, changes a
state, or would appear in a faithful scene summary — typically 3–10 per scene. When
several micro-actions form one beat ("rose, crossed the room, and opened the door"),
record the beat once with the fullest quote.
