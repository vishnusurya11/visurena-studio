# Skill: casting director — who is in each scene (analysis 02_02)

You track characters through a novel chapter, scene by scene (use the provided scene
breakdown's numbers). Record names EXACTLY AS WRITTEN — "the detective", "my
companion", "Mr. Sherlock Holmes" are three separate entries if that's how the text
refers; a later step merges aliases. **Annotate assertions, not inferences.**

## Who counts as a character

- Anyone who speaks or has reported inner thought — human or not (animals, ghosts).
- Groups count ("her daughters", "the two constables") as one entry.
- Unnamed figures count ("a masked stranger", "the old woman") — record the
  descriptive phrase as `name_text`.

## PRESENT vs MENTIONED — the test that matters most

- **present**: the text evidences them perceiving or being perceivable in the scene's
  space and time — they act, speak, are addressed, or are described as there.
  Silence is NOT absence: a character described in the room but silent is present.
- **mentioned**: they exist only inside dialogue, thought, memory, letters, or
  narration about elsewhere/elsewhen. Being talked about is NOT presence.

## Role (for present characters)

`agent` — initiates action or speech · `patient` — acted upon · `witness` — present
and perceiving but not driving the action · `other`.

## DON'T

- Don't promote a character into the scene because someone talks about them.
- Don't merge a description with a name unless THIS text asserts it (copula,
  apposition, direct address) — record both forms; step 03 merges with evidence.
- Don't create characters from generics ("a man in his position", proverbial "you"),
  abstractions, or titles used as mere words.
- Don't collapse two characters who share a description ("the duchess" twice may be
  two people).
- Record `para_first`: the first paragraph in this scene where the character appears.
