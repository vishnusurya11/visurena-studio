# A silent gap belongs to the silent shot

QC walls the longest stretch of the cut with no voice in it at 6.0 s. Episode
8 came in at 6.81 s and the obvious lever was to hand: the shots on either
side of the gap held too long, so trim their codas. It worked on the number --
6.81 → 5.93 -- and it cost a take. Shot 16 lost 0.9 s of coda, froze at 51 %,
and froze worse on a fresh seed (`frozen_starts.md`).

The gap was never the neighbours'. It was shot 17's: the episode's one silent
shot, sitting in the middle of it, contributing its whole length to the hole
by definition. Giving shot 17 a line closed the gap outright -- 6.81 → 4.04 --
and moved the silence to shot 3, the public-house lantern, which is short and
has a line on either side of it and so opens a hole of 1.7 s.

**The rule.** When QC names a silent gap, find the silent shot inside it
first. Three cures, in this order:

1. **Give the silent shot a line.** The gap stops existing. Free.
2. **Move the silence** to a short shot with lines on both sides. The contract
   wants at least one silent shot; it does not care which.
3. **Only then** trim a coda -- and never a moving shot's, because the coda is
   the room its move finishes in.

The fault class is the one this pipeline keeps producing: two numbers, each
fine alone, whose relationship nobody measured. Here it was the gap (measured
on the cut) and the coda (written for the shot). Trimming one to fix the other
treated them as the same number. They are not.
