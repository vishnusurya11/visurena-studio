# Episode 12 deep DQ: why it is bad, and why every gate said PASS

2026-09-26. Four read-only analysts (picture, story, motion, audio) over
`episodes/ep12/cut/master_iter7.mp4` (published https://youtu.be/Vl_f2GUM7cA),
with ep10 and ep11 measured the same way as the house baseline. Evidence frames
and measurement scripts: the session scratchpad `dq_picture/`, `dq_story/`,
`dq_motion/`, `dq_audio/`.

**The brick.** Every gate in the chain measures FORMAT (words heard, loudness,
frame-exact cuts, a face at some height, a block that changed) and none measures
DEPICTION or PERFORMANCE (is the thing the line says on screen, does it move the
way the plan says, does the voice do what the line does, does the world make a
sound). A pipeline built only of format gates ships whatever the models give it,
green. ep12 is not an outlier: ep10 and ep11 share every systemic fault below
(ep11 is worse on motion: 10 dead shots to ep12's 5).

## 1. Story: the chapter's climax is not in the episode (my plan)

The plan was hand-authored in this session. Its lines stop at paragraph 57 of 69
(the Martian falls). Paragraphs 58-69 -- the chapter's TITLE events -- are absent:

- the Heat-Rays destroying Weybridge and Shepperton, the towing path swept, the
  river boiled (64-68); the Heat-Ray is not shown once in the episode;
- the scalding wave, the narrator staggering out half blinded onto the gravel in
  full view of the Martians (68);
- the Martian foot a score of yards from his head, the four carrying the wreck
  away, "by a miracle I had escaped" (69).

The episode plays as a victory with a shrug. Also: the narrator's goal (Leatherhead,
his wife) is never said; the guns -- the plan's own QUESTION -- are never on screen;
the lieutenant, the orchid man and the crowd are cropped out of every exchange; the
artilleryman is shown at the river after the kill, where the book has lost him, and
his "bows and arrows" line is moved to after the kill, which reverses it.

Cause: the answer shot was picked before the chapter was read to its end; shots were
allotted in paragraph order with no weight budget; the one-named-person-per-frame
rule removed every exchange's other half. ep10 and ep11 share the shape (ending on a
dialogue button + a silent shot).

## 2. Sound: there is no sound design at all

- `scripts/episode/assemble.py:846` mixes with an EMPTY cue list (`[]`). No guns,
  no shell burst, no falling tower, no Heat-Ray, no water, no crowd, no hooves, no
  "Aloo". Outside the voice the only event >12 dB above its surroundings is the
  title sting.
- The bed is the Holmes solo violin (`studio/episode_bed.py`, owner 2026-09-13 for
  A Study in Scarlet). It gets QUIETER at the climax (-41 dB under the guns), drops
  to digital silence 112.5-115.3 s while he runs into the river, and the `grave`
  tone restarts from bar one on the answer shot.
- Every shout is read as calm narration: `say_lines.py:166` passes the quiet design
  clip as IndexTTS2's EMOTION reference too. "Get under the water!" is the flattest
  line in the episode (F0 94 Hz, p95 104). Lines are then peak- and loudness-levelled
  identically, so a whisper and a scream are the same level.
- The narrator's speaker check is `ok: false` (worst pair 0.648) and QC passed;
  the artilleryman ("low military voice") sits 9 semitones above the narrator.

## 3. Motion: a third of the runtime is animated stills, and the meter cannot see it

- Five shots are dead air (T06, T10, T14, T18, T22; ~36 s of 155): 70-100 % of
  frames static by whole-frame optical flow. T07, T23 nearly as bad.
- `studio/motion_gate.py` calls a frame alive when its BUSIEST 24x24 block moved
  (STILL 1.2): a smoke puff, a glint or a mouth is enough. All 24 takes read 0 %
  frozen. It also resizes 1:1 frames to 192x336 portrait.
- `camera_ok` is false on 16 of 24 takes and is read by nothing (take_dq.py). No
  gate checks pan/tilt direction or that the scripted gesture happened.
- T15 morphs (willows into a riverbank); T16 holds 2.4 s then spasms in 0.6 s.
- Editing is metronomic: every shot = one line + 2.2 s tail; the climax cuts at the
  same pace as the opening.

## 4. Picture: two styles in one film, cut-out staging, no locked machine

- The character sheets are low-poly/Pixar 3D (faceted faces and hands); the places
  are painterly-realistic. People look pasted onto paintings; the narrator drifts
  between the two looks (T16 vs T20).
- The board composes characters as cut-outs over high-angle establishing plates: no
  ground contact, broken scale (T02, T16, T22 -- a giant artilleryman standing ON
  the river).
- The fighting-machine sheet is a white sci-fi droid with a green visor, not Wells's
  brazen hood, and it is loosely bound: T15 droids, T21 a "headless" machine with its
  head on and an arm, T23 grey pods. Seven shots graded D or F (T06, T07, T14, T15,
  T16, T21, T22).
- 768 px takes upscaled to 1536 go soft in wide/atmospheric shots (T14, T23).

## Why the owner-facing checks all said PASS

| Gate | What it measured | What it could not see |
|---|---|---|
| QC | LUFS, true peak, lines heard, cut timing, frame-exact edit | silence where sound belongs, flat shouts, the story |
| take gate frozen-share | block-max diff | a still with one moving puff |
| camera_ok | computed | read by nothing |
| panel/content gates | named subject present in its cell | whether the frame depicts the line (a paddling tripod passes as "the guns fired") |
| master eye | shadow, face height, board resemblance, repeats, a turn verb | the missing climax, sound, performance, style clash |
| speaker check | `ok: false` | nothing reads it as a failure |
| me | a 40-frame contact sheet at 200 px | everything above |

## What has to change (ordered by what the viewer notices first)

1. **Sound design stage.** A plan field of cues per shot (gun, burst, crash, ray,
   water, crowd, howl) + an ambience bed per setup, mixed from a local SFX library;
   a QC row that fails a shot whose plan names a sound and whose master is silent
   there. (Generalisable: a plan-driven SFX spotter for AI video -- publishable.)
2. **Performance reaches the voice.** An emotion field per line; an emotion reference
   clip per register (shout, whisper, dread) instead of the reading clip; no
   per-line levelling that erases dynamics; a prosody row (pitch lift on `!` lines).
3. **The plan reads the whole chapter.** A coverage row in the plan battery: the last
   line's paragraph vs the chapter's last paragraph; the turn and answer chosen after
   the whole chapter is read; shots budgeted by weight, not order.
4. **One look.** Re-render the character sheets in the places' painterly style (or the
   reverse); lock a Wellsian fighting-machine sheet (brass hood, three jointed legs,
   tentacles) and bind it on every machine shot.
5. **A stillness meter that sees stills.** Whole-frame, motion-compensated flow;
   thresholds fitted on the owner-judged episodes; `camera_ok` read; a shot-length
   ceiling for static takes. (Generalisable: an I2V motion-quality benchmark.)
6. **Staging.** Eye-level cameras for people; two-person frames for exchanges; no
   cut-outs over aerial plates.
